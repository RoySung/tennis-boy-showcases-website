# Tennis Boy 01. — Standing & Motion

Minimal English character exhibit set in a warm, low-saturation Japanese tennis center. Small Character Showcase wordmark, a single title and pose label, and a large centered character. Credit: Created by RoySung.

Four quiet orientation shortcuts, reset and native angle slider. Responsive mobile stage prioritizes touch drag. No marketing copy, halo or technical status labels. The interface uses one fixed warm-daylight palette; there is no theme switch. A generated panoramic tennis-center environment remains quieter and less saturated than the character. Standing has no horizontal panorama movement, only subtle cloud, atmosphere and edge-prop drift; reduced-motion keeps it fully still.

Rotation uses time-based easing and circular angle-driven adjacent-frame blending. The dissolve occupies the middle 12% of each adjacent angle interval (44–56%), with smoothstep easing. Whole-frame horizontal translation brings measured torso anchors closer, capped at 0.6% of image width and weighted by the blend; original views return to their original position at interval endpoints. No scaling, vertical displacement, or local warping is applied. Reduced motion uses the nearest view with zero translation. When rotation settles, any remaining blend resolves to the nearest single view within 70ms, so pausing cannot retain a double silhouette. Respect reduced motion. Preserve the eleven supplied transparent PNGs without modification.

The standing character appears directly in the tennis-center scene without a pedestal or added contact shadow. Touch input never drives scenery motion; reduced motion keeps the environment still.

## V2 layered environment

The stage layers a clean tennis-center panorama (distant mountains, courts and paving), transparent white clouds, a floodlight near the fence, the character, a ground-level tennis ball, and edge planters. The panorama in `public/environment/tennis-center-loop-v1.png` is retained as the style reference; the V2 background removes its clouds, lamps and planters so independently moving layers do not duplicate them. New artwork keeps the original warm-daylight painterly treatment. See `docs/environment-assets-v2.json` for generation prompts, dimensions and checksums.

In 360° View the panorama remains still while clouds and edge props drift very slightly; one tennis ball flies in from beyond the left edge and exits beyond the right. Each new crossing randomly selects one of the other two energy-decay patterns, preventing an immediate repeat: a power bounce with a faster 4.2-ball-diameter first rebound, four low skidding rebounds, or a high lob. The lob's first rebound reaches 6.5 ball diameters over 38% of the crossing cycle, followed by a 2.8-ball-diameter rebound over 26%, keeping its higher arc without excessive hang time. Every pattern loses height, spacing and impact squash toward the right edge. During Motion playback the panorama scrolls at the existing base rate, clouds move more slowly, and floodlights scroll between the distant panorama and the faster foreground planters. Floodlights repeat offscreen to keep the moving scene continuous; the same left-to-right ball action runs faster and stays in time with playback. Scenery playback follows 0.5×, 1× and 1.5× speed, pauses with the walk, and stays paused while its tab is inactive. With reduced motion, all environment layers and the ball are still. Foreground decorations never intercept stage input and stay quiet enough to preserve the character as the focal point.


## Motion exhibit

A centered, keyboard-accessible two-tab switch sits above the stage. The initial tab is 360° View. Motion uses the subtitle “Walking · 36°” and shares the same tennis-center world. Its background moves right at an 80 px/s reference speed while the character remains fixed, and scales with 0.5× / 1× / 1.5× playback. On mobile the title, tabs, stage and controls form one vertical stack. The Motion stage allows vertical scrolling and has no rotation gesture.

The control row contains Previous frame, Play/Pause and Next frame, followed by a native frame slider (01–16), 0.5× / 1× / 1.5× speed buttons and a short keyboard hint. Space toggles playback while the stage is focused; arrows step and pause; Home/End select the first/last frame. Tabs use Left/Right/Home/End keyboard navigation. Focus is visible throughout.

Walking uses a fixed ordered list of sixteen independent 1254 × 1254 transparent PNGs at 16 fps. All frames preload and decode on the first visit to Motion. One image is drawn per frame without dissolves, scaling changes, image warping or pose interpolation. Fractional-frame timing preserves speed across display refresh rates. Hidden documents reset the timestamp and stop requesting frames; returning to the document resumes without catching up elapsed hidden time.

First entry autoplays only after every frame is decoded; reduced-motion preference starts paused but permits explicit playback. Enabling reduced motion during playback pauses it. Frame stepping/scrubbing pauses. Leaving Motion preserves its position and returns paused; leaving 360° stops automatic rotation and preserves its current angle. The two viewer components stay mounted after their first use, preserving their independent state.

Failed, malformed or stalled images produce a visible error and retry button. Playback controls remain disabled until all frames are decoded. Retry creates a fresh batch, and cancellation prevents stale requests from completing a newer load. Generated images remain separate from the CSS environment and have no ground shadow. Background-extraction masks provide alpha while the original character RGB pixels remain unchanged.

The current artwork expands the eight-pose prototype to sixteen frames: contact, weight acceptance, down, rising to passing, passing, low forward swing, up/reach and pre-contact for each side. At 16 fps, the two-step cycle still lasts one second. Revised up poses and intermediate poses allow the pelvis, shoulders, neck and head to rise and fall together, with knee and ankle articulation rather than a whole-sprite translation. The subsequent gait refinement revises 005, 006, 011–014 and 016: support feet follow smoother screen-space travel, the two passing/low-swing poses use more comparable knee lift, and the last pre-contact pose bridges the body rise back into contact. Perspective and heel roll remain visible; left/right symmetry means comparable phase, clearance and body rhythm, not mirrored images or zero horizontal foot movement in a walk in place. Identity, head size, fixed camera and support-foot placement remain visual acceptance criteria. Inspect 016→001 and both weight-transfer arcs at full and half speed. `npm run verify:gait` checks fixed pixel proxies for the observed discontinuities; it does not certify physical foot locking. Measurements and verification are recorded in `docs/qa/walking-gait-refinement.json`, `docs/qa/walking-body-motion.json` and `docs/qa/walking-validation.md`.

## Scene proportions

Standing and Motion use the same square character container: 71% of stage height, capped at 92% of stage width. Standing is anchored 5% above the bottom; Motion is lowered to 2% to compensate for the walking artwork's larger transparent foot margin. The panorama renders at 112% of stage height and is offset 8% above the stage, cropping excess sky while enlarging the fence and court to a more believable scale. Props scale with stage height instead of viewport width: the floodlight is 74% (72% on mobile), planters are 21% (18% on mobile), and the tennis ball is 5.2%. The floodlight is inset 10% from its tile's right edge (12% on mobile) and raised to 13% above the stage bottom (14% on mobile), keeping its base clear of the planter. During Motion its loop duration follows the rendered panorama width so its screen-space speed stays 1.2× the distant panorama at every stage size, while remaining slower than the planters. The ball travels on the paving in front of the character but behind the planters, so a planter occludes it when their paths cross. Ball and planters use full layer opacity; transparent image pixels retain their original antialiased edges. Original image proportions and character pixels remain unchanged.
