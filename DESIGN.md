# Tennis Boy 01. — Standing & Motion

Minimal English character exhibit on a light or dark neutral background with restrained geometric accents. Small Character Showcase wordmark, a single title and pose label, large centered character with a separate soft shadow. Credit: Created by RoySung.

Four quiet orientation shortcuts, reset and native angle slider. Responsive mobile stage prioritizes touch drag. No marketing copy, halo or technical status labels. A low elliptical pedestal with a matte top, shaded edge and contact shadow grounds the character. A top-right Light/Dark button switches the complete palette. Initial theme follows the system; explicit choice persists locally. Low-contrast monochrome spheres, a CSS 3D cube and a tilted triangular prism frame the character without intercepting interactions. Smaller background solids at 28% opacity keep the character dominant. Pointer movement adds restrained 5–16px depth-dependent parallax and gentle cube rotation; leaving the viewport resets the composition. Touch and reduced-motion preferences keep the backdrop still.

Rotation uses time-based easing and circular angle-driven adjacent-frame blending. The dissolve occupies the middle 12% of each adjacent angle interval (44–56%), with smoothstep easing. Whole-frame horizontal translation brings measured torso anchors closer, capped at 0.6% of image width and weighted by the blend; original views return to their original position at interval endpoints. No scaling, vertical displacement, or local warping is applied. Reduced motion uses the nearest view with zero translation. When rotation settles, any remaining blend resolves to the nearest single view within 70ms, so pausing cannot retain a double silhouette. Respect reduced motion. Preserve the eleven supplied transparent PNGs without modification.

The pedestal has a deeper 60px elliptical top and a 14px rim, with room around both shoes. The character, contact shadow and pedestal share a restrained 7px horizontal / 4px vertical pointer parallax so the feet stay planted. The shared pointer easing also drives the background; touch and reduced-motion keep the exhibit still.


## Motion exhibit

A centered, keyboard-accessible two-tab switch sits above the stage. The initial tab is 360° View. Motion uses the subtitle “Walking · 36°” and keeps the original theme, typography, geometric scenery. Its transparent canvas follows the active theme; Motion omits the pedestal and contact shadow and keeps its exhibit still. On mobile the title, tabs, stage and controls form one vertical stack. The Motion stage allows vertical scrolling and has no rotation gesture.

The control row contains Previous frame, Play/Pause and Next frame, followed by a native frame slider (01–16), 0.5× / 1× / 1.5× speed buttons and a short keyboard hint. Space toggles playback while the stage is focused; arrows step and pause; Home/End select the first/last frame. Tabs use Left/Right/Home/End keyboard navigation. Focus is visible throughout.

Walking uses a fixed ordered list of sixteen independent 1254 × 1254 transparent PNGs at 16 fps. All frames preload and decode on the first visit to Motion. One image is drawn per frame without dissolves, scaling changes, image warping or pose interpolation. Fractional-frame timing preserves speed across display refresh rates. Hidden documents reset the timestamp and stop requesting frames; returning to the document resumes without catching up elapsed hidden time.

First entry autoplays only after every frame is decoded; reduced-motion preference starts paused but permits explicit playback. Enabling reduced motion during playback pauses it. Frame stepping/scrubbing pauses. Leaving Motion preserves its position and returns paused; leaving 360° stops automatic rotation and preserves its current angle. The two viewer components stay mounted after their first use, preserving their independent state.

Failed, malformed or stalled images produce a visible error and retry button. Playback controls remain disabled until all frames are decoded. Retry creates a fresh batch, and cancellation prevents stale requests from completing a newer load. Generated images remain separate from the CSS environment and have no ground shadow. Background-extraction masks provide alpha while the original character RGB pixels remain unchanged.

The current artwork expands the eight-pose prototype to sixteen frames: contact, weight acceptance, down, rising to passing, passing, low forward swing, up/reach and pre-contact for each side. At 16 fps, the two-step cycle still lasts one second. Revised up poses and intermediate poses allow the pelvis, shoulders, neck and head to rise and fall together, with knee and ankle articulation rather than a whole-sprite translation. The subsequent gait refinement revises 005, 006, 011–014 and 016: support feet follow smoother screen-space travel, the two passing/low-swing poses use more comparable knee lift, and the last pre-contact pose bridges the body rise back into contact. Perspective and heel roll remain visible; left/right symmetry means comparable phase, clearance and body rhythm, not mirrored images or zero horizontal foot movement in a walk in place. Identity, head size, fixed camera and support-foot placement remain visual acceptance criteria. Inspect 016→001 and both weight-transfer arcs at full and half speed. `npm run verify:gait` checks fixed pixel proxies for the observed discontinuities; it does not certify physical foot locking. Measurements and verification are recorded in `docs/qa/walking-gait-refinement.json`, `docs/qa/walking-body-motion.json` and `docs/qa/walking-validation.md`.
